What changed in this edition — the powers of the blood and the bound are set at last:
  • The werewolf's climb is whole at last: a true ten-rung Wolf Altar questline (gold, mutton, Tongue of Dog, the Horn of the Hunt, then kills, howls, tamings and the taking of a life), the wolf and wolfman shapes switched by the Moon Charm, their distinct powers (dig-for-bones, the feast, the paralysing and pack-calling howls, armour-rending claws), and the level-ten bite that spreads the curse.   • The Infernal witch's dominion is whole: thralls now hold their pact — they will not turn on their master and they avenge any blow struck against you — may be sacrificed for their nature, and that stolen nature wards you unbidden, snuffing flame, granting breath underwater, and swallowing the lightning-bolt.
  • The vampire's climb is cut to its full height: Resist Sun truly holds back the daylight from the fifth rung, and the late trials — the three-village pilgrimage and the caging and bleeding of villagers — open the eighth and ninth. A Rite to Remove Vampirism now offers a coven's cure for the afflicted.
  • The living coven stands at last: wandering Coven Witches set you a trial — fetch an ingredient, or slay a creature they conjure and bring back its remains — before swearing to your coven (six at most). The sworn now fight at your side, hurling brews at whatever you strike and at any foe that strikes you, and never at you or their sisters; the bond holds across sleep and distance, and the Seer Stone reads your coven's number and any trial set you.
  • The triggered curse is woven into the craft. A brew taught with a Zombie Head is no longer drunk or thrown at a body but laid — upon loose items, or upon a button, lever, pressure plate, or door — to wait, unseen, for the next unwary hand. A trap, patient and faithful.
  • The Witch's Cauldron has answered its last withholdings: creeping liquid brews and drifting gas, the brew bucket, casting straight from the pot over Tongue of Dog, the Gas Mask that turns aside foul fumes, and the Brew of Disease with the Purified-Milk cure that lifts it.
  • A new and uncanny companion: the Creaking familiar of the pale groves, bound by the Rite of the Creakening — still and watchful, moving only when no eye is on it.
  • The Crystal Ball truly scries now: hold a Taglock Kit, a Bound Waystone, or a Blooded Waystone and the ball bears your sight out for a sustained minute — a real vision that follows a soul or hovers above a waystone's place, spending altar power as it holds. Dropped bound and Blooded Waystones also transpose the living and loose things near them out of an Otherwhere circle.
  • The earlier printing's additions remain: the spectral coven raised from five remembered tales; the early chapters reordered so the Oven precedes Seeds and Mutandis; the truer Crystal Ball prophecies; the Hellhound and Mindrake; the Morsmordre glyph; the Specialist Gear chapter; Worldgen & Structures; and the fuller Item ID and reference-book tables.

Part I

The Witch's Path

The craft has no grand ceremony of initiation. You begin when you begin. These chapters cover the practical requirements.

Before you proceed — keep this in mind as you work through the first seven chapters:
  • Gather oak and birch saplings, eggs, Mandrake Root, and the four Anointing Paste seeds
  • Build a Witches' Oven with Clay Jars in the jar slot before attempting the Altar
  • Burn oak saplings for Exhale of the Horned One; burn birch saplings for Breath of the Goddess
  • Craft Anointing Paste, convert a vanilla Cauldron, and make Mutandis
  • Use Mutandis to secure Rowan Saplings if you have not found a Rowan tree naturally
  • Build the Altar (2×3 vertical); wait for the red cloth to appear
  • Surround it with plants — aim for at least 400 power before brewing anything
  • Build a Kettle over fire, within 15 blocks of the altar; test it with one brew
  • Draw your first chalk circle and activate a small rite
  • Craft a Taglock Kit; collect your own taglock from a bed
  • When ready: 8,000 altar power for Familiar binding

Chapter 1: The Witches' OvenEarly ↑ Contents

Not everything useful comes from a kettle. Some ingredients must be rendered down first — reduced, transformed, made into something the craft can actually use. The Witches' Oven is where that happens. It smolders saplings into ash, coaxes herbs into fumes, and generally works its patience on raw materials until they are ready.

Build it before all else. The Altar recipe needs two fume ingredients only the Oven can produce, and the very first Mutandis — the next chapter's work — is compounded from the Wood Ash and Exhale of the Horned One the Oven renders. Learn the smoke first, then the green. This is the intended order of things.

Witches' Oven: Iron Bars (top center); Iron Ingot + Iron Ingot + Iron Ingot (middle row); Iron Ingot + Iron Bars + Iron Ingot (bottom row). Three iron ingots short of an anvil, one iron bar more interesting.

The Oven functions like a furnace: input slot, fuel slot, output slot. The critical difference is the jar slot. Always keep a Clay Jar there — fume by-products not captured are lost permanently. The Oven will not tell you this is happening.

Clay Jar: Four clay balls in a 2×2 square.

Fume Funnels

You can attach up to three Fume Funnels to the Oven's sides. Each one speeds processing and improves fume yield. Filtered Fume Funnels accept only one fume type — useful when you want a specific ingredient in quantity and do not care what else the Oven produces. Three filtered funnels on a dedicated oven is a reasonable late investment.

Chapter 2: The Old Way — Seeds and MutandisEarly ↑ Contents

The craft begins not with building, but with looking. Before the altar exists, before the kettle hangs over fire, there is a walk through the world with open hands — collecting what the land offers. The seeds you gather now are what make the rest possible. Four of them unlock the Witch's Cauldron. One root and one egg unlock mutation itself. Without these, the Rowan tree remains a rumour in cold forests.

What to Gather First

Everything below serves a purpose in the first hour. The four seeds are the priority — they make Anointing Paste, which converts a plain Cauldron into a Witch's Cauldron. The Mandrake Root goes into the first Mutandis brew.

Anointing Paste — Converting the Cauldron

Once you have all four seeds, craft Anointing Paste. The recipe is shapeless — put all four seed types into a crafting grid together. You receive four paste.

Anointing Paste (shapeless, crafts 4): Water Artichoke Seeds + Mandrake Seeds + Belladonna Seeds + Snowbell Seeds.

Place a vanilla Cauldron in the world. Right-click it with the Anointing Paste. The Cauldron permanently becomes a Witch's Cauldron — a different block with different behaviour. You need the Witches' Oven running before the next step. See Chapter 1 for the Oven build; come back here when you have Exhale of the Horned One.

Mutandis — The Key to Hidden Plants

Fill the Witch's Cauldron with water (three buckets), place it over a roaring fire (netherrack flame recommended), and wait for it to heat. Drop a Mandrake Root and an Egg into the bowl — toss them in, or right-click. Then right-click the cauldron with Exhale of the Horned One in hand. Exhale is the trigger. This produces six Mutandis.

Mutandis (Witch's Cauldron, heated): Mandrake Root + Egg → right-click with Exhale of the Horned One. Yields 6.

Mutandis is the foundational mutation ingredient. Its primary early use: right-clicking small vanilla plants (grass, ferns, flowers) to transform them into Witchery plant variants. Keep applying it until you see Rowan Saplings — and while you are at it, collect Belladonna, Snowbell, and Blood Poppy from the same process. Mutandis is not rare. Make more whenever you need it.

Six Mutandis goes further than it sounds. One application per plant. Work a 10×10 patch of grass and flowers systematically. Rowan Saplings, Alder Saplings, Hawthorn Saplings, and all the other hidden plants will surface within a few patches.

Once you have Rowan Saplings, proceed to Chapter 2b to grow your grove and prepare for the Altar.

Chapter 2b: The Rowan TreeEarly ↑ Contents

The rowan is unremarkable to look at — a compact tree with reddish bark, prone to growing in cold forests and unlikely hillsides. But it carries something the other trees do not: a sympathy with the unseen world that makes it essential to a practising witch. Its wood builds your altar. Its leaves feed it. Without the rowan, your altar does not begin. If Mutandis has done its work, you have saplings. Now grow the grove.

What to Harvest

  • Rowan Logs — primary resource; 1 log → 4 planks (shapeless). The altar requires them.
  • Rowan Saplings — grow a grove. Your long-term altar power depends on the leaves.
  • Rowan Leaves — break with shears to keep the block intact. +4 power each, cap 50. Better than any vanilla leaf.
A 5×5 canopy of Rowan leaves contributes +200 altar power before you have placed anything else nearby. Plant the grove first, build the altar second, let the leaves grow into position before you power anything demanding.

The Ent — A Note on Restraint

If you fell too many Rowan, Alder, or Hawthorn trees in a short span, a woodland spirit animates one of the remaining trees to defend them. The result — a 200-HP animated tree called an Ent — is hostile, can fling you into the air, and considers your visit a personal insult. Ents are weak to axes and drop Ent Twigs, which are useful later. Harvest sustainably or be ready for the fight.

Chapter 3: The AltarEarly ↑ Contents

If the rowan is your foundation, the altar is your hearth. It gathers magical energy from the natural world around it — trees, crops, flowers, the particular darkness of certain stones — and stores it as power that your brews and rites can spend. Without it, most of the craft does not function.

The altar is a 2-wide × 3-tall × 1-deep multi-block structure. Place six Altar blocks in a vertical rectangle. The structure activates — red cloth appears across its surface — when the last block is placed. The last block placed is the center of its 14-block scanning range. Choose the spot with care, because moving it later is a significant undertaking.

Altar (crafts 3 blocks):
Row 1: Breath of the Goddess · Water Bottle · Exhale of the Horned One
Row 2: Stone Brick · Rowan Wood · Stone Brick
Row 3: Stone Brick · Rowan Wood · Stone Brick
Craft the altar. Then decide where it will live, because the objects around it matter.

How Power Works

The altar scans a 14-block cube continuously, tallying power from the natural blocks and plants within range. Power recharges toward a maximum over time. Rites and brews spend power the moment they complete. The craft rewards those who tend their spaces.

The principle that matters most: variety over quantity. Each block type contributes up to a cap — adding a thousand dirt blocks past the cap of 80 contributes nothing. Spread across many plant and block types to reach the theoretical maximum of roughly 3,980 power. The full table is in Appendix A.

Chapter 4: The KettleEarly ↑ Contents

A kettle is a simple thing: a vessel, a fire, and whatever you choose to put inside it. That last part is where things get interesting. Right-click to add reagents one by one, light a fire beneath it, and it draws power from the nearest altar to complete the working. The ingredients determine what emerges.

Attuned Stone: Vertical 3×1 — Whiff of Magic (top) · Diamond (middle) · Lava Bucket (bottom).

Kettle: Row 1: Stick · String · Stick / Row 2: String · Attuned Stone · String / Row 3: — · Cauldron · —
Iron and a modicum of patience. That is all a kettle needs.

Brewing

  1. Place the Kettle above fire. Netherrack flame is permanent; ordinary fire must not go out mid-brew.
  2. Right-click with a Water Bucket to fill it.
  3. Throw ingredients in one at a time. Order matters for some recipes.
  4. Watch the particles — green swirls: brewing correctly. White swirls: ready to bottle. Black smoke: ruined.
  5. Right-click with an empty Glass Bottle to collect the finished brew.
If the fire goes out, the brew is ruined. If the altar cannot supply power when you bottle, the result is a useless brown liquid. Black swirling particles — not smoke — mean insufficient power. Wait for the altar to recharge and try again.

The kettle must be within approximately 15 blocks of an active altar to draw power. One altar can supply one kettle per brew. Plan your workspace accordingly.

Flying Ointment — An Early Milestone

The Flying Ointment is the first brew that demands real altar power. You will need it for the Enchanted Broom, and eventually you will want the Enchanted Broom. Make it as soon as you have 3,000 power available.

Flying Ointment (needs 3,000 altar power): Redstone Soup + Feather + Potion of Swiftness (8-min extended version) + Wool of Bat + Diamond + Belladonna Flower → 3× Flying Ointment. The Swiftness potion must be the long-duration variant — the regular version does not work.
Wearing a Witch's Hat and Robes while bottling can yield more flasks than the recipe states. The bonus applies throughout the game. Always wear the hat when you intend to bottle in quantity.

Chapter 5: Circle MagicEarly ↑ Contents

Before you can perform a rite, you must prepare the ground for it. The circle is not mere tradition — it is the structure that makes the working possible, a closed space where intention and power accumulate until they are ready to be released. A gap in the ring, even a small one, undoes the whole working. Power spills, reagents are wasted, and the rite fails with the special indignity reserved for careless practitioners.

Chalk Types

Use each according to what you actually need, not what is most impressive. There are four types. Three draw the outer ring; one draws the centre activation glyph.

Ritual Chalk chalk

Standard circles. Most rites — familiar binding, waystones, necromancy, protection, fetishes, curses — begin here. If you are unsure which chalk to use, use this one.

Infernal Chalk chalk_infernal

Demonic and underworld rites. Summoning Demons, Flame Imps, the Wither. Glows orange-red. Use it because the rite requires it, not for atmosphere.

Otherwhere Chalk chalk_otherwhere

Teleportation and End-realm workings. Transposing to waystones, pulling targets, moving ore to surface. Shimmers pale purple.

Golden Chalk chalk_golden

Draws the centre activation glyph only — not the outer ring. Every rite is begun from a Golden Chalk centre: it is the glyph you right-click to fire the circle, and a centre of plain chalk will not answer at all. Keep some always at hand. Crafted via a heated, filled Witch's Cauldron: offer Mandrake Root + Gold Nugget in the bowl, then use Ritual Chalk on the cauldron; costs 2,870 altar power.

Circle Sizes

Three ritual circles, child, and no more:
Small (7×7): 16 glyphs — utility, Eclipse, waystones, charging.
Medium (11×11): 28 glyphs — binding, summoning, infusions.
Large (15×15): 40 glyphs — demons, Shifting Seasons, transposition.

Drawing a Circle

The outer ring uses your circle chalk (Ritual/Infernal/Otherwhere) — that ring's colour decides which rites the circle will hold. The centre glyph is always Golden Chalk, whatever the ring. Right-click each glyph position in sequence — the ring first, then the golden centre last. The circle activates when you right-click that golden centre.

Drawing and Activating

  1. Equip chalk and right-click a flat surface to draw each glyph around the perimeter in sequence.
  2. When the ring closes, you will feel it — the circle is complete.
  3. Throw the required ingredients into the center.
  4. Step into the center and right-click the centre glyph to trigger the rite. This centre glyph must be drawn with Golden Chalk — every rite needs it, and a centre of regular chalk will do nothing at all.
The Circle Talisman stores a drawn circle and redeploys it instantly anywhere. Right-click the center glyph with an empty talisman to store. Right-click any suitable flat surface to redeploy. A genuine convenience when your knees object to drawing the same ring twice. Carry three — one per chalk type, pre-loaded with your most common rites.
Chalk glyphs erase faster with a Broom than anything else. Keep one when setting up several circles in a session.

Your First Rite: Charging an Attuned Stone

The Rite of Charging stores 2,000 altar power in an Attuned Stone. The stone is not consumed — it reverts to uncharged after use. Carry a charged stone for field casting, away from your base.

Rite of Charging — Attuned Stone (paired 7×7 + 11×11 Ritual rings, 2,000 power): Attuned Stone + Glowstone Dust + Redstone Dust + Wood Ash + Quicklime.

Chapter 6: TaglocksEarly ↑ Contents

A taglock is a thread of sympathy connecting a practitioner to their subject. Without it, poppets are merely decorative, curses fall without direction, and certain rites cannot find their target. It is a simple thing to collect and a dangerous thing to lose control of.

Taglock Kit: Glass Bottle + Bone Needle (shapeless, 2 items). A glass bottle and a pin. Simple enough, and deliberately so — the difficulty is in what you do with it afterward.

Chapter 7: FamiliarsMid ↑ Contents

A familiar is not merely a tame animal. It is a companion bound to you through ritual — and it carries part of the working forward with it. You may have one at a time. Choose accordingly.

The binding elevates both animal and witch: the animal gains supernatural resilience; the witch gains passive bonuses, unlocked recipes, and a life-share that is equal parts power and vulnerability. The rite requires a Medium (11×11) Ritual circle, 8,000 altar power, and five specific ingredients.

Ingredients for the Binding

  • Tear of the Goddess — Distillery: Breath of the Goddess + Lapis Lazuli
  • Odour of Purity — Distillery: Diamond + Oil of Vitriol
  • Whiff of Magic — Distillery: Breath of the Goddess + Lapis Lazuli (same process, different output)
  • Diamond
  • Demonic Blood — Distillery: Demon Heart + Diamond Vapor
The rite kills the animal if it fails — insufficient power, broken circle, wrong ingredients. Confirm 8,000 power is stable before you begin. Do not attempt on a newly built altar.

Choosing Your Familiar

Cat

The cat is indifferent to most things and hostile to supernatural intrusion. Your curses find stronger purchase, and others' curses find less on you. Enables the Curse of Corrupt Poppet and Brew of Cursed Leaping. Tame an ocelot — approach slowly, do not run.

Owl

The owl reads the air around you. Broom flight improves substantially with one present; they seem to enjoy the height. Brew of Soaring gives +50% speed (vs. +30% without). Enables Brew of Bodega. Source: mutated from a Wolf — see Chapter 29. Can be spent to create a Flying Monkey. This is permanent.

Toad

The toad is unimpressive until you need brews in quantity. +5% chance for an extra flask per kettle batch. Empowers Brew of Thorns, Sprouting, and Vines. Enables Brew of Frog's Tongue. Tame with Rotten Flesh. Source: mutated from an Ocelot — see Chapter 29.

The Life-Link

Damage you take redirects to your familiar at 10% nearby, 1% at distance. If the familiar dies, you die. If you die, it dies. A dead familiar can be resummoned through the Rite of Summoning — the binding persists. Dismissing a familiar by sneak-clicking it removes all bonuses until you resummon it.

The Creaking — A Familiar of the Pale Garden

Some companions you tame. This one you must first catch unlooking — for it is only ever still when it knows it is watched, and only ever moving when it believes it is not.

Newest and strangest of the bound companions, and not won by the binding above. From the pale, silent groves comes the Creaking — a thing of standing deadwood that freezes the instant a stranger's eye falls on it and stirs only when unwatched. To bind one you do not need the five distillates; instead draw a Medium (11×11) Ritual circle with a wild Creaking standing inside it, and lay at the golden centre a Creaking Heart as the trigger, together with a Resin Clump, a Pale Oak Sapling, Whiff of Magic, Tear of the Goddess, and Mutandis Extremis. This is the Rite of the Creakening.

Once bound it follows, defends, sits on command, and is dismissed by a sneak-click like any familiar, shedding resin as it goes and keeping its uncanny habit of standing dead-still under a watcher's gaze. It is a young working, and its temper is still being learned — keep it as a curiosity of the craft for now, and do not yet lean your whole coven-craft upon it.

See Chapter 29 for how to create Owls and Toads. See Chapter 11 (Rite of Summoning) for resurrecting a dead familiar. See Chapter 14 for poppets that protect both you and your familiar from dying.

Chapter 9b: Custom Brews — The Witch's CauldronLate ↑ Contents

The kettle is for apprentices and the impatient. The cauldron is for those who understand that a potion is not a single note — it is a chord.

Custom Brews are the advanced brewing system. Where the Kettle produces fixed brews from set recipes, the Witch's Cauldron lets you compose entirely custom potions by combining six parameters: Capacity, Power, Duration, Modifier, Dispersal, and Effect. A single custom brew can contain multiple effects simultaneously, with controllable area of effect, lingering duration, and optional ritual casting instead of bottling.

Where the craft stands — the cauldron has woken fully. You can stir in capacity, raise power and duration, invert and conceal an effect, widen it into a thrown splash, hang it as a lingering cloud, pour it as a creeping liquid (Wormwood floods that flow and sink like finite water), loose it as drifting gas, set it as a trigger-curse upon an item or a doorway, and draw on a broad roster of potion- and working-effects — each kept faithful to the old recipe by which the elder witches measured their brews. The drifting gas and liquid brew blocks, the brew bucket that scoops and pours a batch, the glass-flung splash, Gas Mask protection against foul fumes, the Brew of Disease and the Purified-Milk cure that answers it, and casting straight from the pot over Tongue of Dog all answer the hand now. What still settles into its final shape: a few of the grander dynamic workings, the exact temper of the far-aimed ritual-cast (its long pulse, its backfires, its waystone- and taglock-aimed targeting), and the old spell-projectile flights. The Witch's Cauldron itself and its older workings — the chalk upgrades, Mutandis, Mutandis Extremis, Drop of Luck, and Mutating Sprig — have answered the hand since the first.

The Witch's Cauldron

A separate block from the Kettle. Apply Anointing Paste to a vanilla Cauldron placed in the world — right-click with the paste. It requires a roaring fire underneath (netherrack flame recommended) and up to three buckets of water. Fill it with buckets or bottles; rain, too, will raise the water over time.

First thing to make in the Witch's Cauldron: Mutandis — fill and heat the cauldron, drop or right-click a Mandrake Root and an Egg into the bowl, then right-click the cauldron with Exhale of the Horned One. Exhale is the trigger, not a loose offering. This produces six Mutandis, the foundational mutation ingredient and prerequisite for Mutandis Extremis.

Dropping In, or Stirring In — Learn This Before All Else

A reagent flung into the pot and a reagent stirred into it are not the same thing, child, no more than a word spoken and a word meant. The cauldron listens to your hands. Tell it plainly what you intend, or it will decide for you — and it does not decide in your favour.

Mark this well, for more first brews are spoiled here than at any other step. There are two ways a thing may enter the pot, and the cauldron does not treat them alike. Most reagents you simply drop or toss into the bowl, or right-click in, and the heated water draws them under and folds them into the working of its own accord — one at a time, each taken with a small witch-light puff of smoke. Count those puffs. They are the pot telling you it heard.

But certain ingredients already have a calling at the cauldron. Some are triggers that wake a set working — Mutandis and its kin — the moment they touch the brim. Others are offerings the chalk upgrades wait upon, set aside for a purpose older than your brew. Drop one of these and it keeps faith with that older calling; it will not quietly melt into your custom brew, however long you wait. To bend such a reagent to a brew instead, you must say so deliberately: hold it and sneak — crouch — and right-click the cauldron. That stooped, careful gesture is the whole of the spell here; it tells the pot you mean this as a brew reagent and not as a trigger or a gift. The cauldron must be heated and holding water, as for any stirring. Drop when you mean the old working; sneak to stir when you mean the brew. Confuse the two and you will lose both the reagent and your patience.

Exhale of the Horned One, above all. This is the reagent that catches every apprentice. Right-click the cauldron with Exhale in an ordinary hand and you do not stir it — you fire the Mutandis working, for Exhale is its trigger (see the recipe just above). Stand there expecting it to thicken a brew and you will only keep brewing Mutandis and wondering why. When you truly want Exhale in a brew — it quickens the drinking of one — you must sneak and right-click to stir it, exactly as below. Remember the Horned One's breath, and the rest will come easily.
The reagents that must be sneak-stirred — because a plain drop sends them to their other calling — are: Exhale of the Horned One (else it fires Mutandis), Coal, Stone, Dandelion, and Bone Meal (held for their rite and cauldron-offering uses), Blaze Powder and Gold Nugget (held for the Infernal and Golden Chalk offerings), and Reek of Misfortune and Purified Milk (held for their kettle recipes). When in doubt with any reagent that earns its keep two ways, sneak to stir.

The Triggered Curse — Traps and Cursed Things

A curse is patience made cruel. You lay it down and walk away; it waits, perfectly still, for the hand that does not know to fear it.

Add a Zombie Head to a brew and it becomes no longer a thing to drink, nor to dash against a body, but a thing to set. Bottle the triggered brew as a splash and hurl it where you mean the trap to lie:

  • Over loose items — every dropped item the splash washes over is cursed. The next soul to use a cursed item takes one charge of the brew upon themselves, with a breath's cooldown between releases; when the last charge is spent the curse lifts and the thing is clean again.
  • Over a button, lever, pressure plate, or wooden door — the fixture is cursed where it stands, wearing no mark and looking no different than before. The next witch or fool to press, flip, tread upon, or open it receives the brew. Use it again and again and the curse pays out until its charges run dry, then it is only a fixture once more.

Each splash lays a single charge; curse the same thing twice with the same brew and the charges stack. A cat familiar at your side, schooled in the mastery of curses, doubles what each splash lays down. You may also loose a triggered brew straight from the cauldron over Tongue of Dog, cursing the items at its foot without ever drawing a bottle.

A cursed block keeps its own true face — a birch door stays birch, a stone button stone, a snow plate snow. Nothing betrays the working but your own memory of where you laid it. Mark your traps well, child, lest you spring your own and curse no one but yourself.

Boline

A boline is a crescent-shaped ceremonial knife — a real implement used in herbalism and Wicca for centuries. In Witchery it is a multi-purpose tool: it works like shears on plant matter and can harvest things shears cannot (Cobwebs, among others). As a weapon it deals wooden-sword damage with iron-sword durability. It is also required in every Fetish rite and Spectral Stone binding — without a Boline, the spirit craft cannot proceed.

Anointing Paste

Anointing Paste converts a plain vanilla Cauldron into a Witch's Cauldron. Without it, early Mutandis and the later cauldron workings do not exist for you. The recipe requires seed forms of four Witchery plants — collect them while foraging.

Anointing Paste (crafts 4): shapeless — Water Artichoke Seeds + Mandrake Seeds + Belladonna Seeds + Snowbell Seeds. Place a vanilla Cauldron in the world; right-click it with the paste. The Cauldron becomes a Witch's Cauldron permanently.

Necromantic Stone

More than a crafting ingredient: the Necromantic Stone is a control device. Use it to direct undead raised by a Brew of Raising — point it at a mob, player, or block and your undead minions will attack the target or move to that location. It is also the only means of turning an Alluring Skull on and off. The stone is created via the Rite of Necromancy (night only; requires an Attuned Stone as its input).

Once created, handle it with intent. Pointing it at yourself by accident while undead are nearby ends badly.

Spectral Familiar

The Spectral Familiar is unlike the Cat, Owl, and Toad familiars — it is a helping spirit summoned from the Rite of Summoning (7×7 Ritual, 2,000 power: Drop of Luck + Raw Porkchop + Gold Ingot + Arthana) rather than a bound animal. It treats its summoner as having tamed it and behaves as a passive scout.

Its particular gift: give it Coal, any metal ingot, Lapis Lazuli, a Diamond, or an Emerald and it will consume the material and sniff out the nearest ore of that type. Useful for mining expeditions. The warning: do it too many times and it may simply leave with the material you gave it. It has 40 HP and drops Spectral Dust.

The Spectral Familiar is not the same as a bound Familiar (Cat/Owl/Toad). It is a one-time summoned spirit with no life-link, no passive bonuses to the witch, and no persistent bond. When it wanders off or dies, you summon a new one from scratch via the same rite. It is a utility spirit, not a companion.

Alluring Skull

The Alluring Skull is a placed block that draws nearby undead toward itself. Place it, right-click with a Necromantic Stone to activate — all undead in range will move toward the skull. Right-click again with the Stone to deactivate. Break it with the Necromantic Stone to remove it. Useful for corralling raised undead during combat without needing to point the stone constantly.

Chapter 13: Coven MagicLate ↑ Contents

They exist because we exist — and a coven, properly assembled, is more capable than any individual practitioner working alone. Some rites require multiple witches. Some workings are large enough that a single altar cannot supply them unassisted. That is what a coven is for.

Recruiting a Coven Witch

Coven Witches are neutral NPCs found wandering the overworld. An open hand offered to one opens her wares for trade; but to broach the deeper matter — a place in your coven — you must come to her crouched. She will only entertain the notion if you have a Familiar bound; a witch with no familiar she takes for no witch at all.

If she is willing, she sets you a trial. Sometimes it is a simple fetching — a measure of bones, a Demon Heart, mandrake root, graveyard dust, a quartz sphere. Other times she conjures a pet for you to best: a hardy spider or thrall, near a hundred hearts of vigour, set straight upon you. Slay that creature — none other will do — and carry back the eye or the rotten flesh it leaves, which bears her mark. Return the asked-for thing to her, crouched as before, and she swears to your coven.

A coven holds six at the most, and the bond is a lasting one — it survives your sleeping and waking, and the witches wandering out of sight and back. Should you wish to release one, come to her crouched: she will ask you to be certain, and only a second asking sends her on her way.

Using Coven Members

The sworn fight at your side. A witch of your coven hurls her harmful brews at whatever creature you strike, and rounds on any foe that dares strike you — yet she will never loose a brew at you, nor at her sister witches. Between battles they keep their own counsel, often near your home.

The Seer Stone is how you take their measure and call them near. Used plainly it reads your own state — your familiar, the number sworn to your coven, any trial a nearby witch has set you, and what curses ride you. Aimed at another, it reads them instead. Crouch and use it to call a nearby coven witch to your side. And as of old, waking the Heart Glyph at a circle's heart with the Stone gathers the coven to lend their voices to a rite that demands more than one.

The Spectral Coven — A Coven of the Remembered

A witch with no living sisters within a day's walk is not, for that, barred from coven work. There is an older and lonelier way: where you cannot gather the living, you gather the remembered. Scattered across the world, copied out in trembling hands and left to yellow in chest and cellar, lie twelve old village tales — the lives of witches now under the rowans. Find any five of them and the trick is yours: read them over a circle until the air goes the second kind of silent — not empty, but listening — and the coven-shape itself rises to stand with you.

"Do not mistake them for the dead returned. The dead have better things to do than stand in your draughty barn. What rises is the weight of five lives well-remembered, lent to your working as if the sisters stood beside you again."

What answers are five pale, footless witches, drifting a hand's breadth above the chalk. They do not speak and they do not walk; they turn slowly to face the altar and wait. While they linger, the circle counts them as kin — a witch alone may now cast what only a coven could, calling a familiar's beast or any working that demands more than one voice. They keep no altar long, fading of their own accord after some minutes. When they go, simply gather the lives again — tales tire of being told, and so do the told.

Rite of the Gathered Lives — draw a medium Otherwhere circle (9×9 ring, Otherwhere chalk) beside an altar with at least 2,000 power to spend. The Coffin

A vampire does not sleep in a bed. The sun makes ordinary beds inadvisable, and there is a certain indignity to them regardless. The coffin is better: sealed against daylight, appropriate to the condition, and functional. Any player can use it, but for vampires it resolves the daylight problem with characteristic directness.

Chapter 22: The Hobgoblin EcosystemMid ↑ Contents

Hobgoblins are small, territorial, and largely reasonable as long as you do not disturb their patron. They live in woodland huts, they trade in koboldite — a curious metal found only through their commerce — and they can be led around with appropriate items, which they find irresistible. Finding Them

Hobgoblin Huts generate in forest biomes — 5×5×5 oak plank structures with a minimum 32-block spacing and 1–2 residents each. The loot chest inside contains Koboldite Dust. This is worth finding before you start the trade chain.

  1. Coal → Koboldite Dust
  2. Koboldite Dust + Gold Nugget → Koboldite Nugget
  3. Koboldite Nuggets → Koboldite Ingot
  4. Koboldite Ingot → Koboldite Pickaxe

The Statue collects worship from hobgoblins within 16 blocks. Each worship event has a 25% chance of angering nearby hobgoblins named Gulg or Mog into attacking the player. Chapter 23: The Crystal Ball and DivinationLate ↑ Contents

The crystal ball does not tell you what you want to hear. It tells you what it perceives — which is oblique, contextual, and occasionally alarming. Right-click it without a held item, near an altar holding at least 500 power, and it will offer a reading. Whether that reading is a limitation or a mercy is a matter of interpretation.

Crystal Ball (Rite of Infusion, 11×11 Ritual, 2,000 power): Quartz Sphere + Gold Ingot + Happenstance Oil. The ball knows more than you do. This is fine.

Remote Viewing — Scrying

The ball will carry your sight out across the world, if you give it something of the place or person you would see. Stand by an altar with power to spare, hold one of the foci below, and right-click: your body goes still, your held hand empties, and your vision is borne away for about a minute — a sustained scrying that spends altar power (the old five hundred) to open and holds while it lasts.

Predictions and Their Consequences

Used near an active altar (at least 500 power) with an empty hand, the ball fixes a single prophecy upon a nearby soul — usually you, sometimes another who stands too close. Often the world obliges of its own accord — the foretold arrow finds you in some later skirmish, you slip and fall, you are caught in the rain, you break open the very ore the ball named. Chapter 25: The Flying MonkeyMid ↑ Contents

There are witches who keep cats. There are witches who keep owls. And then there are witches who keep flying monkeys, and they tend to have a different kind of reputation.

Creation — and What It Costs

To create a Flying Monkey, you need your Owl Familiar, a tamed Wolf, and Mutandis Extremis. Right-click the Wolf with the Mutandis Extremis while the Owl Familiar is within approximately 5 blocks. Both the Wolf and the Owl are consumed. A Flying Monkey appears.

Waystone Courier

Chapter 26: Rowan SecurityMid ↑ Contents

The Rowan security system locks doors to a specific UUID — only the exact key, or a keyring containing it, can open the door. It is the most secure storage solution in the mod.

When a Rowan Door is placed, the game automatically generates a Rowan Door Key with a unique UUID and gives it to the placing player. No other player can open that door without the matching key or keyring.

Rowan Keyring

Combine two or more Rowan Door Keys in a crafting grid (shapeless) to create a Keyring. The Keyring holds all their UUIDs and counts as a match for any door whose key it contains. One inventory slot; access to every door you have ever keyed into it.

For a coven base: assign each door a key, then distribute keyrings with all base door keys to every member. When a new door goes up, mint one new key and add copies to everyone's rings — or pass the keyring and let them do it themselves.

Chapter 27: Silver — The Hunter's MetalMid ↑ Contents

Silver does not exist as ore. It must be precipitated from gold through the Silver Vat process. This makes it genuinely limited. Plan accordingly.

The Silver Vat

Silver Vat: 4 Iron Ingots (corners of 3×3) + Furnace (center) + Water Bucket.

Place the Silver Vat adjacent to an active Furnace smelting Gold Ore. Right-click the Vat to begin monitoring. Each gold ore smelted has a 25% chance to yield 1 Silver Ingot in the Vat. On average: four gold ore per silver ingot. Run multiple adjacent furnaces, or use Fortune pickaxes for more ore per vein before smelting.

Silver Sword

Silver Sword: 8 Silver Ingots filling the 8 surrounding slots of a 3×3 grid, Golden Sword in the center.

Deals substantially increased damage to werewolves and vampires. Witch Hunters carry these as standard melee weapons. The damage bonus is significant — plan for it if you are transformed.

Silvered Witch Hunter Armor

Each standard Witch Hunter armor piece can be silvered by combining it with 2–3 Silver Ingots in the crafting grid. The silvered variants maintain the same stats while adding silver's anti-supernatural property. Silvered armor still retains all full-set bonus properties of the base Witch Hunter set — including the poppet failure clause. That remains.

Chapter 28: Dream Weavers and Sleep MagicMid ↑ Contents

Chapter 29: Mutation — Growing Owls and ToadsMid ↑ Contents

Owls and Toads do not spawn naturally. Both must be created through the mutation system — a spatial multi-block apparatus using Critter Snares, Grasspers, and Mutandis Extremis. The apparatus layout is the same for both; only the source animal and snare contents differ.

Mutation Apparatus Components

The mutation apparatus requires three items that are themselves created through mutation. Build them in this order before attempting to create an Owl or Toad.

Mutating Sprig

The trigger item. Used on the apparatus cobweb to start a mutation. It also converts Dirt to Mycelium (or Mycelium to Dirt) when left-clicked, and converts submerged Dirt to Clay. Durability is limited — it breaks after a few uses.

Mutating Sprig (Witch's Cauldron, 3,080 altar power): offer Nether Wart + Ent Twig, then use Mutandis Extremis on the heated, filled cauldron → Mutating Sprig.

Critter Snare

A trap that captures and holds a single small mob. Used in the mutation apparatus to hold the source creatures (Bats for Owls, Slimes for Toads). The captured mob stays inside even if you pick up the snare. Sneak-right-click to release the mob without breaking it.

Critter Snare (mutation): Place a Cobweb above a Water source block. Surround it North/South/East/West with Alder Saplings. Lure a Zombie into the cobweb. Use a Mutating Sprig on the cobweb. Each attempt produces Critter Snares at the sapling positions. The Zombie is consumed.

Grassper

A grasping plant that holds an item. Used in the mutation apparatus to hold material components (Mutandis Extremis, Attuned Stone Charged). Right-click a Grassper to insert an item; right-click again to take it back. Also counts as an altar power source (+2 per block, cap 10).

Grassper (mutation): Place a Chest above a Water source block. Surround it North/South/East/West with Tall Grass. Use a Mutating Sprig on the Chest. The Chest and Grass are consumed; Grasspers appear at the grass positions.
Both Critter Snares and Grasspers require the Mutating Sprig to create, and the Sprig itself now uses a live Witch's Cauldron route. Make the sprig first, then use it for the mutation apparatus rather than relying on command-only placeholders.

The Apparatus

  1. Place a Cobweb directly on top of a Water source block.
  2. In the four cardinal directions (N/S/E/W): place Critter Snares. Fill 2–4 of them with the target creature. The number of filled snares equals the number of animals produced.
  3. In the four diagonal directions (NW/SW/SE/NE): place Grasspers. Three hold 3× Mutandis Extremis each. The North-East Grassper holds an Attuned Stone (Charged) instead.
  4. Lure the source animal onto the cobweb.
  5. Use a Mutating Sprig on the cobweb to trigger.

Chapter 33: The Craft in Conflict — Multiplayer and PvPLate ↑ Contents

Witchcraft in a multiplayer world is not merely a progression system — it is an adversarial toolkit. This chapter covers the craft as a weapon and a defense: how to pursue another player through taglock and rite, and how to make yourself as difficult a target as possible.

The Attacker's Toolkit

Step 1 — Acquire the Taglock

Nothing works without the taglock. Prioritize collection before anything else.

  • Bed method — the most reliable. If you have physical access to their bed, right-click it while they sleep. Silent; they receive no notification.
  • Sneak approach — sneak behind them and right-click. Can fail; failed attempts send a notification. Get it right the first time.
  • Blood Poppy — plant a Blood Poppy near a location they frequent. After they take damage nearby, right-click the poppy. Passive; they never know.
  • Leech Chest — found in generated structures. Contents vary; occasionally pre-filled.

Step 2 — Destroy Their Defenses First

A well-prepared witch will have a Voodoo Protection Poppet on a Poppet Shelf. Apply the Curse of Corrupt Poppet before all other offensive rites — this destroys a poppet in their possession. Target the protection poppets; leave the offensive ones for later. This requires a Cat Familiar.

If they have multiple poppets on a shelf, the Curse of Corrupt Poppet destroys one at a time. Apply it repeatedly. They have a finite shelf; you can outlast it.

The Pull Rite — Forced Extraction

Once you have their taglock, the Rite of Transposition (Entity Pull) yanks them to your location regardless of where they are. Set up a prepared kill zone first — summon a Sentinel Fetish or have allies waiting — then pull and let the environment do the work. The 90% Witch Hunter armor resist means you need to cast this multiple times on a well-equipped target, but each cast is cheap relative to what it accomplishes if it lands.

Coven Coordination

Two or more witches attacking the same target are significantly more effective than one. Divide roles: one witch handles the Pull rite; one curses; one has the Sentinel Fetish deployed. The target's attention is split and their counters are finite. Coven witches can assist directly in the kill zone.

The Defender's Toolkit

Taglock Security — Your First Priority

  • Never sleep near strangers; use a coffin in a locked room if possible
  • Keep your bed in a Rowan-door-locked room with a keyring held only by you
  • A Voodoo Protection Poppet on a Poppet Shelf, bound to yourself, counters most curse effects passively — keep it stocked
  • A Poppet Protection Poppet blocks remote destruction of your other poppets

Countering the Pull Rite

The Entity Pull cannot be fully blocked — only resisted. Full Witch Hunter armor gives 90% resistance. That means roughly 1 in 10 casts will land regardless of armor. Countermeasures:

  • Wear full Witch Hunter armor when you know an enemy has your taglock — accept the poppet failure and survive the pulls
  • Spend your taglock — if you suspect someone has it, change beds and collect a fresh taglock of yourself, which does not invalidate the old one but gives you a fresh one to use in your own protective rites
  • Stay deep underground or in the Torment Dimension — Transposition pulls work dimensionally but you have more warning time if you hear the rite activating

Countering Curses

The Rite of Remove Curse clears one curse per cast. Against a persistent attacker who keeps reapplying: maintain a Voodoo Protection Poppet and have allies cast the Rite of Removal on you repeatedly — or outlast their altar power. A Cat Familiar does not prevent incoming curses but makes them easier to remove and harder to land in the first place.

Base Defense — Fetishes and Weavers Used Offensively

Fetishes placed around your base perimeter are your first physical deterrent. A Sentinel Fetish attacks intruders without requiring you to be present. A Disorientation Fetish makes navigation inside your base confusing for anyone who enters without authorization. A Shrieking Fetish adds noise and panic. Place them on walls, in corridors, and at choke points.

A Dream Weaver of Nightmares placed near a sleeping area in your base that an attacker might exploit does not protect you — but it corrupts any beneficial Dream Weavers they might have planted there during a prior visit. Check your walls occasionally.

The Nuclear Option — Demons at Home

A Sentinel Fetish is reliable defense. A summoned Demon bound to a prepared room is less reliable and significantly more dangerous to everyone including you. If you choose to summon Demons for base defense, ensure the binding is tight and the clear space requirement (7×7×4 inside the circle) is architecturally contained. A Demon that breaks containment does not distinguish allies from enemies. That said: it is an option, and experienced attackers will hesitate.

The odds and ends of the craft — belts and slippers, charms and traps, brew vessels and reward gear that fit no other chapter cleanly. Most of these are mid-to-late luxuries: none are required to progress, but each one solves a problem the rest of the craft leaves open.

Biting Belt

The Biting Belt is a legs-slot armor piece made from Impregnated Leather that carries the natural abilities of a Parasytic Louse. It can be loaded with up to two potions: when the wearer is attacked, healing and buff-type potions apply to the wearer, while harm and debuff-type potions apply to the attacker. It is, in other words, a retaliatory potion rig built into your clothing.

The belt is dyeable, invisible while the wearer is invisible (to other players but not mobs), and repairable with 4× Impregnated Leather. Leather-level armor, iron-level durability.

Bark Belt

The Bark Belt is a legs-slot armor piece that grows a natural bark shield when the wearer stands on grass or mycelium. This bark appears as stick icons on the armor bar and absorbs hits — being struck while bark is present costs only 1–2 bark points and negates all damage, unless the attacker uses wooden weapons. Everything except wood is blocked by bark. The tooltip says it all: So this is how an Ent feels.

Bark Belt: Row 1: Brew of Flowing Spirit · Emerald · Brew of Flowing Spirit / Row 2: Ent Twig · Biting Belt · Ent Twig / Row 3: Brew of Flowing Spirit · Creeper Heart · Brew of Flowing Spirit. Requires a Biting Belt as its base.

The bark grows two sticks per eligible armor/clothing piece worn — Witch's Hat, Witch's Robes, and other Impregnated Leather items each add two. If you wear incompatible armor (iron, chainmail) alongside the Belt, bark does not grow over those pieces. Maximum bark accumulates faster when you are standing still on grass. Plan your fights accordingly — the Belt rewards defenders who hold ground.

Wooden weapons bypass bark protection entirely. A well-equipped attacker who knows you wear the Bark Belt will carry a wooden sword. The same applies to Baba Yaga's Thorns — always check what your enemy is swinging before relying on bark.

Beartrap

A placeable trap that captures and holds in place most mobs. Place it on the ground — any mob that walks over it is caught and cannot move until the trap is broken or the mob escapes. Useful for holding live creatures for taglock collection, keeping summoned entities in place while you prepare a circle, or slowing down a pursuing Witch Hunter.

Beartrap: Row 1: Iron Ingot × 3 / Row 2: Shears · Heavy Weighted Pressure Plate · Shears / Row 3: Iron Ingot × 3.

Wolftrap

A silvered Beartrap. It works as an ordinary trap against most mobs, but it also serves the werewolf hunter: leave a silvered Wolftrap open, set beside a Wolf Altar, with a leashed sheep within eight blocks, and wait for a full-moon night. Those conditions lure a fresh Wolfman to the area; when the beast wanders onto the trap it is caught and turned infectious, so the next villager it savages becomes a Were-Villager — the curse spreads from a single penned sheep. A werewolf player who steps on a silvered trap is snared too. Unlike the Beartrap, a broken Wolftrap drops nothing — silver does not come back to you cheaply.

Witch's Footwear

A witch is known by her shoes as much as her hat, and these three pairs each earn their place. All sit in the clothing slot, all count toward what your Bark Belt can shelter, and all mend with Impregnated Leather — so wear them without fear of wearing them out.

Ruby Slippers

There is no place like home. With Ruby Slippers worn and a player infusion active, say "there's no place like home" in chat to be carried away. If a dropped Bound Waystone lies within three blocks, the slippers prefer it — spending 40 infusion energy (80 across dimensions), consuming the offered Waystone, and dropping you beside the bound point on a one-minute cooldown. With no Waystone present, they fall back to your bed or spawn point, spending 80 energy (120 across dimensions) on a separate thirty-minute cooldown. Both cooldowns are written into your character and survive logout. Rarely — about one waystone trip in a hundred — the magic slips and blinks you to a random safe spot within 500 blocks instead.

Icy Slippers

While worn, every step freezes the water beneath you to ice and hardens lava to obsidian (the lava conversion costs 10% durability). Icy Slippers are also the key to Perpetual Ice pressure plates — only a wearer triggers them. Registered under both witchery:icy_slippers and the older witchery:ice_slippers; either behaves identically.

Seeping Shoes

The humblest of the three — a clothing-slot shoe the craft counts as proper witch's garb, so it adds to your Bark Belt's shelter. No grand trick to it; some gear earns its keep quietly, and there is no shame in that.

Construction Tools

Staff of Duplication

An epic staff for the witch who would rather build by will than by hand. Touch it to one block to set a corner, touch a second to drink the whole box of blocks between them into the staff. Thereafter each use stamps that captured shape down a block above wherever you point, turning a quarter-turn each time — through 0°, 90°, 180°, then 270° — so you may face your copies whichever way you like. Sneak-use sets it back to square; sneak-use in empty air pours the stored shape out and forgets it. Be reasonable in what you capture — nothing larger than thirty-two blocks to a side, or some eight thousand cells in all, and it will refuse a greedier bite. Mind, too, that empty air in the pattern stamps as emptiness: it will scour away whatever stood where it lands.

Duplication Grenade

Hurl it and a twin of you leaps up where it shatters — your face, your name, your every gesture — and the wolves of the world turn to chase it instead. The decoy carries nothing of value and unlocks no doors; it is a lie made flesh for ten seconds, and ten seconds is often all you need to be elsewhere. It falls to earth as a Quartz Sphere. Keep up to sixteen, and throw them as you would a Sun Grenade.

Universal Antidote

A small mercy in a bottle — drink it and Poison and Wither both let go their grip, and nothing else is disturbed. The glass returns to you after, as with any potion. Two come from a Null Catalyst steeped with a pair of Poison potions. Keep one about you; the witch who throws Brew of Poison is the witch most likely to catch a stray draught of her own.

A village that has lived near the craft long enough learns to wall itself. The Stone Circle

Now and again, far from any village and well back from river and sea, you will come upon a ring of standing stones gathered about a small altar. Do not mistake it for ruin. The Vanishing Witch Hut

There is a hut, pupil, that will not be watched. A stone-capped cottage to the eye, with a cauldron-hearth and an old woman keeping to it — but it has the temper of a wild thing, and like a wild thing it will not move while it is being looked at. Stand within its walls and it stays. Keep it in your sight and it stays. But step out of its door, turn your back, let a fold of the land or a stand of trees come between you and it — and when next you look, the clearing is bare. It has gone elsewhere.

Mostly it has only slipped to some near spot, a stone's throw off through the wood. But now and again it does a stranger thing: it crosses clean out of the waking world and into the dream-country, and stands there in the very same place a sleeper would walk to. If you would chase it across that threshold, you must go the way of dreams to follow.

It is never a tidy leaving. Where the hut stood you will find its leavings — a grey scatter of ash on the air, a few loose planks, old cobwebs the wind worries at — and the ground beneath shows through untouched, for the craft takes only its own timber with it and leaves your earth as it was. If you stood close as it went, you will have heard the old woman murmur, soft as a draught under a door: "We may not meet again." She keeps close to her hearth and will not wander out to badger you, so if you would trade with her, do your dealing indoors while you have her.

Should you wish to keep her — and her hut — from slipping away, there is the old remedy, the same our grandmothers laid at every door against every uninvited thing: salt. Draw three marks of Witches' Salt across the doorway threshold, on the ground at the door's mouth, and the hut is held fast. No hut crosses a salt line. While the line lies unbroken she cannot leave; scuff it away and she is free to vanish again.

Witches' Salt is no rare treasure. Render it from kelp dried in your Witches' Oven — the sea gives up its salt readily enough to the heat — or buy a pinch from any Coven Witch for a single emerald. A small handful drawn at the door is all it takes.

You may now come upon a vanishing hut on your own — it settles, uncommon and shy, among the trees of the forest and out along the edges of the swamp. For testing, the operator's commands serve as well: /witchery setup vanishing_hut settles one nearby; /witchery goto vanishing_hut walks you to the last one known; /witchery hut move forces it to slip even while you stare; /witchery hut watch tells you whether it is presently held, observed, or occupied. If you are testing the salt remedy, lay the line directly across the visible door threshold, three Witches' Salt marks wide.

Chapter 32: Rules Worth KnowingEndgame ↑ Contents

Blocks That Do Not Power the Altar

The following do not contribute, despite reasonable expectation that they should:

  • Double Tall Grass, Large Fern — the 2-block-high variants
  • Lilac, Peony, Rosebush, Sunflower — double-tall decorative flowers
  • Mindrake, Wolfsbane, Wormwood — Witchery plant blocks

Crop Growth Stage Is Irrelevant

Crops contribute +4 power whether planted 5 seconds ago or fully mature. You do not need to harvest or maintain them. Plant them; put a glass ceiling above to prevent growth; move on.

The Pentacle Multiplier Is Multiplicative

Adding the Pentacle does not add ×2 to your recharge. It doubles the total accumulated recharge from all other sources. Base(1) + Player Head(3) = ×4 recharge. Adding Pentacle: ×8. Add the skull upgrades first, then the Pentacle.

The Arthana Extends Outward Distribution Only

Many practitioners place the Arthana expecting to draw power from farther away. This does not happen. The Arthana only extends the range at which the altar can push power outward to kettles. It does not affect gathering range. Only use it if your kettle placement genuinely exceeds the base distribution distance.

The Witch Hunter Set Breaks Your Own Poppets

Wearing all four Witch Hunter pieces causes every protection poppet bound to you to fail with 100% certainty. The shelf scans for your poppets and triggers nothing. If you wear the set, remove your poppets or accept the trade. The set is genuinely useful against curse attacks. It is not compatible with poppet-based protection.

Silver Is 25% Average, Not Guaranteed

Four gold ore per silver ingot on average. Run multiple furnaces adjacent to one Silver Vat, or use Fortune III on ore before smelting. Both accelerate the production rate. Neither makes it fast. Plan ahead.

JEI Does Not Show Circle Magic

The JEI plugin covers Kettle, Oven, Distillery, Spinning Wheel, and Brazier recipes. Circle Magic rites are intentionally excluded — they are meant to be discovered through the Witches' Primer or documentation like this. If you cannot find a recipe in JEI, it is almost certainly a circle rite.

The Attuned Stone (Charged) Is Returned After Use

When used in a rite as a power substitute, the Charged Stone is not consumed — it reverts to an uncharged Attuned Stone and returns to your inventory. Charge it at base; carry it to the field; use it; recharge it next visit. This makes the Rite of Charging one of the most practical investments in the early game.

Demon Heart Treefyd Upgrades Are Permanent

Using a Creeper Heart on a Treefyd permanently raises it to 100 HP and 4 damage. Using a Demon Heart raises it to 150 HP and 8 damage. These upgrades do not stack and cannot be reversed. Use a Demon Heart for the maximum result. A Demon Heart Treefyd is a credible guard with the health of a mid-tier boss.

Taglock Failure Notifies the Target

If you attempt to covertly taglock another player by sneak-approaching them and fail, they receive a chat notification. Repeated failures tell them exactly what you are trying to do. Get it right the first time, or use the bed method instead.

The Flying Monkey Cannot Be Resummoned

The Rite of Summoning works on Familiars that died. A Flying Monkey was never bound as a Familiar — it was created through mutation. If it dies, it is gone. Creating a new one requires a new Owl Familiar sacrifice. This is worth knowing before you take it into Torment.

The Primer is no longer a single fixed volume; it is a book that grows. It opens with one chapter — Getting Started — always at hand, and eleven further chapters that stay shut until you earn them.
